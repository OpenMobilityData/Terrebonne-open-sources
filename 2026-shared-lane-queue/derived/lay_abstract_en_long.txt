Lay summary

In the winter layout the borough proposes for rue de Terrebonne, cyclists going the same way as the cars would ride in the traffic lane, between two rows of parked cars. That lane is too narrow for a car to pass a cyclist with the one metre the Highway Safety Code requires, so a driver who catches up has to stay behind. This note estimates how many cars would line up behind one cyclist at the winter morning rush.

The borough's own counter at Kensington shows about 120 cars an hour at the busiest morning hour in winter, and up to about 160 on a busy day. If a cyclist rides at 15 km/h and cars would otherwise average 25 km/h, a few cars catch up every kilometre, and a few of them turn off at the cross streets. The result: a cyclist typically has two or three cars behind by the time they reach Girouard, from about one on a quiet morning to about eight on a busy morning with a slow cyclist on snow.

Most drivers never meet a cyclist: about 12 of the 120 cars of the peak hour would end up behind one, each losing at most about a minute and a half per kilometre. The speeds are assumptions, not measurements, so the figures are a range rather than a count.
