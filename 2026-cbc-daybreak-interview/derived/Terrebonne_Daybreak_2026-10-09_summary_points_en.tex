{\large\bfseries CBC \emph{Daybreak}, October 9, 2026: the councillor's statements checked against the record}\hfill{\small October~16, 2026}

\vspace{-0.2em}
{\small Summary points of the fact check, verbatim. Full document, sources, page references and the transcript:\\ \href{\repourl/tree/main/2026-cbc-daybreak-interview}{\repourl/tree/main/2026-cbc-daybreak-interview}.}
\vspace{0.4em}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,itemsep=0.3em,leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}\textbf{``Less than one an hour'' \cliptime{03:11} is the smallest reading the tabulated data allow, and a rate is the wrong measure.} The borough's counter at Kensington recorded 4{,}891 bicycle passages between December~1 and March~31: 40.4 a day, 1.68 an hour over 24 hours, both directions (7{,}460 over the City's official winter season for bike lanes, November~16 to March~31). The figure holds only for one direction averaged over the whole day and night (eastbound 0.96, westbound 0.73). On weekdays in the City's winter period for bike lanes, November~16 to March~31, the counter averaged 7.9 an hour at 08:00 and 6.3 at 16:00, and no daytime hour fell below 2.3. What bears on safety is not the rate but the number of trips exposed: 4{,}891 over the four months, 2{,}783 of them eastbound.\end{minipage}\vspace{0.35em}
  \item \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}\textbf{The ``8'' \cliptime{07:03} is a six-hour sum at one intersection on one day under a winter weather advisory, and the same slide carried higher figures.} His explanation, ``the sum of all the hours during the rush hour… six hours,'' matches the slide \pg{62}: Grand, Terrebonne axis, January~29, 2025, 06:30--09:30 and 15:30--18:30 \pg{34}. Not said: the count is one day's, taken while an Environment Canada advisory urged residents to postpone non-essential travel; the slide gives 10 at Cavendish; the twelve-hour count at Grand was 12 \pg{85}; and the borough's own counter, whose table went to the consultant on August~28 \pg{19}, gives 40 a day for the same months. On October~6 the figure was ``8 passage… 4 cyclists.''\end{minipage}\vspace{0.35em}
  \item \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}\textbf{The counter he called ``the source of truth'' \cliptime{07:19} is not on the open-data portal where he placed it.} The City's \emph{Compteurs cyclistes permanents} dataset lists 128 sites, 77 of them Eco-Counter; none is on Terrebonne. The only Terrebonne site is a City detector at Girouard whose one Terrebonne channel counts westbound traffic, from October~23, 2025. The borough's Eco-Counter loop at Kensington is not on the portal, and the borough had not mentioned it in public before a resident obtained its record under access request 2025-697 (answered December~19, 2025); its data have reached the public only through that request and requests 2026-300 and 2026-581.\end{minipage}\vspace{0.35em}
  \item \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}\textbf{``From 40 a day to 30 a day during December to March'' \cliptime{07:34} leaves out March, the busiest of the four months.} The borough's Kensington counter, both directions: December 40.0, January 32.7, February 34.9 a day, and March 53.5, above the range he gave and inside the season he proposes to convert. Over the four months the lane carried 40.4 a day.\end{minipage}\vspace{0.35em}
  \item \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}\textbf{``We have the data for many years'' \cliptime{03:33} is a single continuous winter and a handful of single-day counts.} Continuous counting on Terrebonne began on July~26, 2025 (open-data detector: October~23, 2025). Before that: camera counts at intersections on single days (Miovision, November 2024 and January~29, 2025), EXP's one day of counting on October~4, 2022 in the two peak periods \exppg{13}, and Telraam counts to December 2024 \pg{65}. The borough's own deck averages five counts for motor vehicles \pg{60} and relies on one day for bicycles, the day under the advisory. On October~6: ``data for 3 years in a row.''\end{minipage}\vspace{0.35em}
  \item \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}\textbf{``About 250 parking spots'' removed \cliptime{01:08} is close to the record; the study dates from 2023, the lane from 2024.} The 2023 EXP study counts 475 spaces on Terrebonne, 375 once the 5-metre clearances are applied, and 171 in the retained design \exppg{100}: a reduction of 304, or 204 against the 375. No as-built count is in the record. In 2020 a press report gave 250 spaces removed by that year's temporary lanes (\emph{Métro}, September~1, 2020).\end{minipage}\vspace{0.35em}
  \item \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}\textbf{``At least 50 to 60 parking spots'' \cliptime{01:28} is not referenced in any document released, rests on no measurement, and sits oddly beside the full length of Terrebonne, which he confirmed the change would cover.} The figure appears nowhere in the 508 pages of request 2026-581, which contain no inventory, no occupancy count and no estimate of spaces created; by his own account it is ``a bit too early'' and ``a few rounds'' with the services remain. Asked whether the whole stretch is concerned, he said yes \cliptime{09:01}. On EXP's figures the side to be converted held about 190 spaces after clearances; 50 to 60 over 2.45~km is about one space for every 40 to 50~m of lane removed. The only occupancy survey on record is EXP's, two days in autumn 2022, in the evening: 35--70\% on weekdays \exppg{54}.\end{minipage}\vspace{0.35em}
  \item \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}\textbf{The approval steps he described \cliptime{08:19} mean that City council must vote to undo part of what it approved in 2024, while the City's own safety analysis of the options stays withheld.} ``We vote here at the borough, then a vote at the Conseil Municipal, then it comes back to the borough'' is how the lane was approved: CA24 170018 (February~5, 2024), CM24 0179 (February~19), CA24 170131 (June~3). City council's December sitting is on the 14th and 15th; no borough or City agenda through October~19 carries the item. The central-city documents in the file, the City's June~17 analysis (``plusieurs compromis importants… sécurité'' \pg{6}) and the fire department's opinion \pg{5}, both withheld, are not mentioned in the interview. The 2024 marking was scheduled for June; this change is for December.\end{minipage}\vspace{0.35em}
  \item \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}\textbf{``So for now, we're looking at it in a way that will make it permanent'' \cliptime{12:22} is the third description of the same measure in two months, after ``mesure transitoire'' in the borough's file and ``a test'' at council, and the word is ambiguous.} The service request of August~20 calls it ``mesure transitoire pour l'hiver 2026-2027'' \pg{4}; on October~6 it was ``a test''; the August~13 mandate was a year-round layout, a two-way lane on one side with parking restored \pg{1}. The host's question concerned the winter change; the answer can mean a removal every winter or the year-round layout, and the interview does not say which. His stated trigger for revisiting it: winter bicycle traffic doubling.\end{minipage}\vspace{0.35em}
  \item \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}\textbf{``The final study… still need to be submitted to us'' \cliptime{00:38} came three days after the announcement and a week after the note was due: on his own account, the outcome was announced before the study commissioned to assess it was in hand.} EXP's final note was due on October~2 \pg{12}; a draft was transmitted on September~17 \pg{167}; the outcome was announced on October~6; the note is withheld as part of a decision process still under way (s.~39). A request for the final note is pending (answer due November~7).\end{minipage}\vspace{0.35em}
  \item \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}\textbf{``Reduce vehicle traffic by two-thirds'' \cliptime{09:56} is right; but the cars that remain would now pass cyclists in a single lane hemmed in by parked cars on both sides, where a lawful pass is not possible.} 6{,}300 vehicles a day in 2022 between Cavendish and Grand \exppg{41}; 1{,}375 a day on the Kensington counter's first year ($-78$\,\%). Under the winter layout a cyclist in the converted direction rides in the single driving lane between two rows of parked cars. The 2024 design plans give that lane 3.0 to 4.0~m, and the borough's own engineers, putting the councillors' concepts to the City in June, described a driving lane of that kind, ``coincée entre deux bandes de stationnement,'' as ``très étroite (moins de 4 mètres)'' and expected the fire department ``pourrait s'y opposer'' \pg{8}. A cyclist riding 1.5~m from the parked cars to stay clear of opening doors, which in this layout open towards riders coming from behind, plus the one metre the \emph{Code de la sécurité routière} requires of a passing driver, leaves at most 1.5~m for a vehicle 1.8 to 2~m wide: a driver cannot pass a cyclist lawfully in that lane, and snow banked along the parked cars narrows it further. Before 2024 the street had a second lane and a centre line to cross.\end{minipage}\vspace{0.35em}
  \item \begin{minipage}[t]{\linewidth}\textbf{Compared with the October~6 council meeting.} In his address to the borough council three days earlier, the councillor described the data as ``the official data provided by the borough'' (on \emph{Daybreak}: ``the open data website''); the winter change as a 99\,\% drop, ``from 700 during the good months to 8'' (on \emph{Daybreak}: 30 to 40 a day, and the 8 as a six-hour sum); ``data for 3 years in a row'' (``many years''); ``more than 30 meetings'' (``more than 40''); and the measure as ``a test'' (``for now… permanent''). No number of parking spaces was given in the address. The statements are set side by side in section~3.\end{minipage}\vspace{0.35em}
\end{enumerate}
