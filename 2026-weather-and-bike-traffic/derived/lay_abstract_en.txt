Lay summary

How much does the weather change cycling on rue de Terrebonne? This note uses every count available: citizen-operated camera sensors at King Edward and Draper, the borough's counter at Kensington, the City's detector at Girouard, and trips at the BIXI station at Grand Boulevard, with City counters elsewhere for comparison. Weather is from Trudeau airport; counts run August 2024 to November 2026.

Temperature matters most. Each degree warmer brings 5 to 6 per cent more bicycles, up to about 20 °C, where the numbers stop rising. A day at 0 °C sees 30 to 43 per cent of the bicycles of the same day at 20 °C; a day at −10 °C, 17 to 28 per cent. There is no temperature below which cycling stops.

A day with 2 to 10 mm of rain has 14 to 22 per cent fewer bicycles than a dry day at the same temperature; snow on the ground goes with 31 to 47 per cent fewer at three of the four counters. Darkness matters too: at the same evening hour and temperature, the borough's counter records 27 per cent fewer bicycles after sunset.

Cold, snow and short days arrive together, so much of the winter drop could be either weather or season. Days when the City loaded snow look like other winter days, but are too few to be sure. Year to year, with weather set aside, the counters disagree: one camera counted about 27 per cent more in autumn 2026 than in its first year, the other 12 per cent fewer. Two to three years of counts, with gaps, cannot show whether cycling on the street is rising or falling.

The cameras count only in daylight, the other counters day and night, so only percentage changes are compared. The note makes no recommendation.
