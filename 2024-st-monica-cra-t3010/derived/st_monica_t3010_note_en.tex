\section*{Abstract}

\textbf{Hypothesis tested.} That the protected bike lane built on rue de Terrebonne in May--June 2024 had a measurable effect on the parish's finances. \textbf{Material.} The public financial section of the parish's seventeen T3010 returns, fiscal years 2008 to 2024 (calendar years), as the CRA released them. The 2024 return is the only one whose year includes the lane, for its last seven months; the 2025 return, the first full year, was not yet published on October 23, 2026. \textbf{What the 2024 return shows.} Total revenue of \$250,718, within the range of the five preceding years (\$202,900 to \$276,981), 6~\% below 2023 and 4~\% below 2022, above 2020 and 2021. On the revenue lines closest to the claims made in the debate: tax-receipted gifts \$201,562, 2~\% below 2023 and above 2020, 2021 and 2022; other gifts without receipt \$18,311, below 2022 and 2023 and above 2020 and 2021; rental income \$15,339, level with 2022 and 2023; « other revenue » (the line that named masses, funerals and weddings in 2011) \$15,506, higher than in any year since 2014, but described in the 2024 return as faith-education fees and votive candles, not funerals or weddings. The return has no line for weddings or funerals; the line that once carried them has been nearly empty since 2015, and the sums paid on those occasions can sit on four different lines according to the parish's own practice (section~\ref{sec:4650}). Expenditures of \$269,373 exceeded revenue by \$18,655, the third consecutive year of expenditures above revenue after 2022 (\$31,771) and 2023 (\$8,240), and the seventh of the seventeen years; the increase of that gap from 2023 to 2024 (\$10,415) is smaller than the increase of the compensation line (\$15,783). Cash and short-term investments rose to \$197,111 and total liabilities to \$180,109, both the highest since 2011 or of the series. \textbf{Conclusion.} The 2024 return shows no figure that stands apart from the year-to-year variation of 2019--2023, during which tax-receipted gifts moved by 2 to 15~\% between consecutive years and total revenue by 3 to 28~\%; the deficits precede the lane. Against the twelve other Catholic parishes of the same postal areas (section~\ref{sec:peers}), the parish's change from 2023 to 2024 ($-$6~\% on gifts, $-$6~\% on total revenue) sits below the peers' median ($+$1~\% and $+$5~\%) and inside the middle half of their changes; the parish's gap to the peers' median opened in 2023, before the lane. \textbf{Detection threshold.} \input{detection_sentence_en.tex} A measurable impact is therefore neither established nor excluded by this record: it would have to be large, of the order of a quarter of a year's gifts, to show in the 2024 return. What would allow a stronger test: the 2025 return, read the same way against the peers; and monthly figures, which the parish published in its bulletins until March 2023 and not since (section~\ref{sec:statements}; where statement and return can be compared, 2019, 2020 and 2022, they agree to the dollar).

\section{Purpose and scope}

The parish of St Monica (La fabrique de la paroisse de Sainte-Monique, 6405 rue de Terrebonne) is a registered charity. Like every registered charity in Canada it files a yearly information return, form T3010, with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), and the financial section of that return is public. The parish's building stands on rue de Terrebonne, on the stretch where a protected bike lane was built in 2024, and the parish's situation has been raised in the public debate on that lane. This note documents the parish's returns as the CRA released them, explains how the tables and charts in this folder are produced from them, and reads the charts in the terms of the return itself. It does not attribute any figure to any cause; the returns do not carry that information.

The data cover fiscal years ending December 31, from 2008 to 2024: seventeen returns. Years 2008 to 2018 are on file from a data request answered by the CRA on December 6, 2024; years 2019 to 2024 are row extracts of the CRA's yearly open-data files, fetched on October 23, 2026 (section~\ref{sec:prov}). A transcription of 2019--2023 made from the CRA's Charities Listings « quick view » in late 2024 served as an interim source until then; it was checked against the files and withdrawn (section~\ref{sec:interim}). The 2024 return is the first whose fiscal year includes the months after the lane was built (May--June 2024); the 2025 return was not yet published on October 23, 2026.

\section{The T3010 return}

Form T3010 has a financial section (Section~D for charities reporting on the short form, Schedule~6 for the others) whose entries are numbered. The numbers used here are the CRA's own line numbers; the labels are in \texttt{derived/line\_labels.csv} with a status column: \emph{checked} where the line's meaning is confirmed by the data on file (a total that sums, a text line that names its content, a figure that matches the parish's own published balance sheet), \emph{dictionary} where the label is that of the CRA's \emph{List of charities data dictionary} (version 2.0, kept with the 2024 dataset in \texttt{raw/open-canada/references/}), \emph{quick\_view} where it is the wording of the Charities Listings page as transcribed in 2024, \emph{form} where the label is taken from the form and not independently confirmed. The CRA publishes the form and its guide (T4033) on canada.ca. Version 27 of the form, used for fiscal years 2023 and 2024, adds sub-lines that repeat part of an existing line (4101, cash and bank accounts included in 4100; 4157, land and buildings used for the charity's programs, included in 4155; 4576, income from impact investments included in 4580); they are kept in the long table and not added to any total.

The lines used in the figures are:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{4700} total revenue; \textbf{4500} the total eligible amount of gifts for which the charity issued tax receipts; \textbf{4530} other gifts (no receipt); \textbf{4650} other revenue not already included, which line 4655 describes in 2011 as « Masses, funeral, weddings etc »; \textbf{4610} gross income from the rental of land or buildings; \textbf{4580} interest and investment income; \textbf{4540}, \textbf{4550}, \textbf{4560} revenue from the federal, provincial and municipal governments;
\item \textbf{5100} total expenditures; \textbf{4880} total compensation; \textbf{4850} occupancy costs; \textbf{4840} office supplies and expenses; \textbf{4860} professional and consulting fees; \textbf{4920} all other expenditures, which line 4930 describes in 2011 as « Contributions to diocese GST \& QST » and in 2012 as « GST \& QST »;
\item \textbf{4200} total assets; \textbf{4100} cash, bank accounts and short-term investments; \textbf{4140} long-term investments; \textbf{4155} land and buildings in Canada (on the 2008 form the same figure sits on line 4160); \textbf{4350} total liabilities, with \textbf{4300} accounts payable and \textbf{4310} deferred revenue; \textbf{4950} total expenditures excluding gifts to qualified donees and \textbf{5050} those gifts, whose sum is line 5100.
\end{itemize}

\section{Provenance}\label{sec:prov}

\subsection{Years 2008 to 2018: the CRA's reply of December 6, 2024}

On November 10, 2024 the author asked the CRA's Charities Directorate, through the « Request publicly available data from the List of charities » web form, for the return data of the parish (business number 130341852RR0001) for the tax years 2008 to 2018 inclusive: general information, programs, the financial information (Section~D and Schedule~6), Schedule~5 and the directors' listing. The Directorate replied by e-mail on December 6, 2024 with four zip files of comma-separated values and this statement of the data's dates:

\begin{quote}\small
« You will also find the publicly available Basic contact information, General information, New and ongoing programs, Financial information, Non-cash gifts / gifts in kind (Schedule 5) and Directors, trustees, and like officials listings (Form T1235) data from the registered charity information return (T3010) for 2008 tax years as of September 7, 2017, the 2009 tax year as of June 30, 2018, the 2010 to 2012 tax years as of April 30, 2019, for the 2013 to 2016 tax years as of April 30, 2024 and for 2017 to 2018 tax year, as of October 31, 2024. »
\end{quote}

and this disclaimer:

\begin{quote}\small
« The information in this package contains data that the charitable organization sent to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). Please note that this data may have been manually entered by the CRA, and it has not necessarily been verified for accuracy or completeness by the CRA's Charities Directorate. »
\end{quote}

The files are kept unchanged in \path{raw/cra-charities-listings-2024-12-06/<year>/}, with the CRA's code lists in \texttt{references/} and the charity's listing record of October 31, 2024. Two kinds of file from the reply are not reproduced: the directors' listings (names of individuals) and the basic contact files (the 2008 one names the parish's contact person). The « as of » dates above are carried into every derived table as the \texttt{as\_of} column.

\subsection{Years 2019 to 2024: the CRA's open-data files}\label{sec:open}

The CRA publishes the T3010 data of every registered charity on the Government of Canada's open-data portal, one dataset per fiscal year (« \textless year\textgreater\ List of charities », Open Government Licence -- Canada), as a set of CSV files: identification, general information (sections A to C), programs, the financial section (Section D and Schedule 6), the schedules, and the directors' listings. Each file holds every charity (the 2024 financial file is 21 MB). On October 23, 2026 the portal listed one dataset for each year from 1990 to 2024 and none for 2025; the six datasets 2019--2024 had last been modified on October 4, 2026. Their identifiers are in \texttt{derived/open\_canada\_datasets.csv}.

The script \texttt{derived/fetch\_open\_data.py} asks the portal's API for a dataset's resources, downloads the financial, general-information, identification and program files, keeps the header and the parish's row of each, and stores them in \path{raw/open-canada/<year>/} with the full file's URL, size, SHA-256 hash, modification date and download time in \texttt{SOURCES.tsv}; the files about directors, trustees and like officials are never downloaded. These are extracts of a larger file and are labelled as such. Each financial extract carries one row for the parish, with the fiscal period end, the form version (24 for 2019--2020, 25 for 2021--2022, 27 for 2023--2024) and the section used (Schedule 6 every year). The CRA's data dictionary and code list, downloaded from the 2024 dataset, are in \texttt{raw/open-canada/references/}. The « as of » date carried into the tables for these years is the dataset's modification date. The portal also holds the years already on file from the e-mailed reply; the parish's rows for 2015 to 2018, read from the portal for the comparison of section~\ref{sec:peers}, are identical to the e-mailed files on every line (the portal leaves blank two lines the e-mailed files give as 0.00).

\subsection{The interim transcription and its check}\label{sec:interim}

Until the files were fetched, the 2019--2023 values in this folder were the author's transcription of the CRA's Charities Listings « quick view » (November--December 2024), kept in \texttt{derived/manual\_entries.csv} and marked as manual. On October 23, 2026 the build compared the 120 transcribed values with the files: 113 were identical; six differed (2019 occupancy 57,267 for 57,262; 2020 cash 159,474 for 159,574 and interest 710 for 701; 2023 accounts payable and total liabilities 2,131 for 2,123; 2021 total assets 814,638 for 246,995, the transcription having carried a land-and-buildings line that the 2021 file does not have); one line was in the transcription only (2021 land and buildings) and eight were in the files only (2019 other assets 9,723; line 4950 of each year; the 2023 sub-lines 4101 and 4576). The record is \texttt{derived/manual\_vintage\_check\_2026-10-23.txt}; the transcription was then removed and every figure in the folder comes from a CRA file. The mechanism stays: a value typed from the quick view goes in \texttt{manual\_entries.csv} with its date, the build prefers a file whenever both exist and reports every difference.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\texttt{derived/build.py} does everything from the raw files:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.6em]
\item reads every numbered field of the financial files (the 2008 reply is a single « T3010 Return » file; from 2009 the financial section is its own file) into one long table, \texttt{t3010\_lines.csv}: fiscal year, line, value, text (for the lines that carry a description), form identifier, source, « as of » date, file;
\item resolves one value per year and line, preferring the e-mailed CRA file, then the open-data extract, then a manual entry if any, and reports any disagreement between sources;
\item writes \texttt{t3010\_summary.csv}, one row per year with the lines listed in section~2, the land-and-buildings figure, and the difference between total revenue and total expenditures;
\item checks that line 4950 plus line 5050 equals line 5100 and that the asset lines sum to line 4200, and writes what it found in \texttt{build\_report.txt};
\item runs \texttt{peer\_comparison.py} (section~\ref{sec:peers}), \texttt{detection\_threshold.py} (section~\ref{sec:threshold}) and \texttt{parish\_statements.py} (section~\ref{sec:statements}) and draws the ten charts in English and French (SVG, PNG and PDF) and writes the table of section~\ref{sec:figures} in both languages, then stamps the version of this note and of its French version and compiles them.
\end{enumerate}

Charts (ten, each in English and French): thin bars, a fixed order of colours checked for colour-vision deficiency with the validator of the chart-design method used across these repositories, a legend on every chart with more than one series, one axis per chart (thousands of dollars), the source line and the build date printed under each. In the stacked charts, « other lines » is the total line less the lines shown, so that each bar's height is the return's own total. The checks find one arithmetic gap in the seventeen returns: in 2018 the four asset lines present sum to \$750,031 while line 4200 reads \$750,051. The two gaps reported while the transcription was in use (2019 and 2020) were transcription gaps and are gone (section~\ref{sec:interim}).

\subsection{The line that named masses, funerals and weddings}\label{sec:4650}

\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/other_revenue_4650_en.pdf}\\[-2pt]
{\footnotesize Line 4650 by year, with the description the parish filed on line 4655 where it filed one. The 2023 and 2024 descriptions (recovered expenses; the Faith First program and vigil lights) do not name funerals or weddings; only the 2011 description does.}\end{center}

A frequent assertion in the debate is that the lane has reduced the parish's revenue from weddings and funerals. The return has no line for weddings or funerals. The closest line is 4650, « other revenue not already included », which the parish described on line 4655 in 2011 as « Masses, funeral, weddings etc »; no other year before 2023 carries a description. Its values are \$44,517 (2008), \$45,642 (2009), \$63,217 (2010), \$38,840 (2011), \$42,443 (2012), \$34,943 (2013) and \$32,913 (2014); then \$2,387 in 2015, none in 2016, \$3,365 in 2017, none from 2018 to 2022, \$4,943 in 2023 (line 4655: « recovery of expenses ») and \$15,506 in 2024 (line 4655, as filed: « Faith First, Virgil lights », that is the parish's faith-education program for children, whose sessions its bulletins announce each autumn, and the votive candles lit in the church; « Virgil » for vigil). By the parish's own descriptions, then, the 2023 and 2024 amounts on this line are recovered expenses, program fees and candle offerings, not revenue from funerals or weddings; only the 2011 description names those. The year the line falls from \$32,913 to \$2,387, 2015, is the first year with a value on line 4530, other gifts without receipt (\$24,600). The returns do not say under which line the revenue from masses, funerals and weddings has been reported since 2015.

The parish's own income statement for 2019 and 2020, published in its bulletin of November 14, 2021, carries an « Other income » line of \$83,157 (2019) and \$66,260 (2020) with the footnote « Includes Hall Rentals, bequests, funerals, weddings, etc. ». The returns for those two years carry no value on line 4650, \$8,925 and \$1,175 on rental income (4610), \$34,530 and \$6,602 on gifts without receipt (4530) and \$229,425 and \$195,512 on receipted gifts (4500). The revenue the parish groups as « other income » is thus spread, in the return, over lines the return does not break down further.

The CRA's guide to the return (T4033) defines the lines as follows. Line 4500: « the total eligible amount of gifts received by the charity during the fiscal period for which official donation receipts have been or will be issued ». Line 4530: « the total amount of all other gifts for which official donation receipts were not issued », including « gifts for which the charity did not issue official donation receipts because it could not identify the donor, such as anonymous gifts ». Line 4630: revenue « from fundraising activities for which official donation receipts were not issued, including the gross amounts from activities carried on by the charity (for example, collection boxes and loose collections) ». Line 4610: « the gross income received or earned by the charity from renting its land and buildings », including « income from leasing out surplus space such as in a church parking lot ». Line 4650: « the total of all other revenue received or earned by the charity that is not already included in the amounts above », including sales-tax rebates and the rental of equipment. A sum paid on the occasion of a wedding or a funeral can therefore appear on line 4500 if the parish issues a receipt for it, on line 4530 if it treats it as a gift without receipt, on line 4610 if it is a rental of the hall or the church, or on line 4650; the return does not say which. The CRA's glossary defines a gift as « a voluntary transfer of property without valuable consideration to the donor », and allows an advantage to the donor of up to 80~\% of the amount without the transfer ceasing to be a gift.

Why some gifts carry no receipt, in the CRA's words: asked whether a registered charity has to issue official donation receipts for the gifts it receives, the CRA answers « No. Receipting carries with it a certain administrative burden. Therefore, registered charities may choose to issue receipts according to certain criteria, or they may choose not to issue receipts at all. Some registered charities set minimum donation thresholds for receipting. Others do not provide receipts during certain fundraising events. » A receipt also requires an identified donor: the guide's example for line 4530 is the anonymous gift, and loose collections go to line 4630. For a parish, the weekend collection thus splits between envelopes (receipted, line 4500) and loose cash (lines 4530 or 4630), and a sum given at a funeral or a wedding is receipted or not according to the parish's own practice, which the return does not record.

For the question raised in the debate, the record therefore gives: a line that carried masses, funerals and weddings from 2008 to 2014 at \$32,913 to \$63,217 a year, nearly empty since 2015 while a line of unreceipted gifts appeared; and, for the years since, three lines that may carry that revenue without separating it, whose sum is \$43,455 (2019), \$7,777 (2020), \$12,737 (2021), \$62,351 (2022), \$50,084 (2023) and \$49,156 (2024): lines 4530, 4610 and 4650 added. The returns contain no count of weddings or funerals and no revenue figure attributable to them alone for any year.

\section{Comparison with the other parishes of the area}\label{sec:peers}

A change in one parish's figures can only be read against what the parishes around it did in the same years. The CRA's identification file of the 2024 dataset lists every registered charity with its address; the Catholic parishes (« fabriques ») whose postal code begins with H3T, H3X, H3Z, H4A, H4B, H4V or H4W, that is Côte-des-Neiges, Snowdon, Westmount, Notre-Dame-de-Grâce and Côte-Saint-Luc, are twelve besides Sainte-Monique: Notre-Dame-des-Neiges, Saint-Kevin, Saint-Antonin, Saint-Malachie, Saint-Léon, Ascension of Our Lord, Notre-Dame-de-Grâce, Saint Augustine of Canterbury (these two at one address in the 2024 file), Saint-Raymond, Saint-Ignace-de-Loyola, Sainte-Catherine-de-Sienne and Saint-Richard (\texttt{derived/peer\_parishes.csv}). The script \texttt{derived/fetch\_peer\_parishes.py} keeps their rows of the financial file of each year from 2015 to 2024 in \path{raw/open-canada-peers/<year>/} (\texttt{SOURCES.tsv} as above); all thirteen are present in all ten years. \texttt{derived/peer\_comparison.py} writes the long table (\texttt{peer\_comparison\_long.csv}), the indexed table and the table of changes, and the three charts below.

Two measures are compared. Gifts are the two gift lines added, 4500 and 4530, because the share receipted differs from parish to parish: in 2024 it is 92~\% at Sainte-Monique and from 39~\% to 100~\% among the twelve others, so neither line alone is comparable. Total revenue is line 4700. Each parish is indexed to its own 2017--2019 mean, so that parishes of very different size (total revenue from \$105,748 to \$768,204 in 2024) can be read on one scale; the subject's line is drawn over the twelve others, their median and their middle half.

What the comparison shows. On gifts, Sainte-Monique stood at 79 in 2024 against its 2017--2019 mean, the peers' median at 104 and their middle half between 71 and 122; the parish was above the peers' median in 2022 (85 against 72), below it in 2023 (84 against 93) and further below in 2024. On total revenue, 88 in 2024 against a median of 106 and a middle half of 101 to 144; above the median in 2022 (91 against 92, level), below in 2023 (94 against 110) and 2024. From 2023 to 2024 the parish's gifts fell by 6.1~\% and its total revenue by 6.0~\%; among the twelve peers, gifts moved by a median of $+$0.8~\% (middle half $-$11.2 to $+$19.9~\%; seven rose, five fell, three by more than Sainte-Monique's) and total revenue by a median of $+$5.0~\% (middle half $-$6.5 to $+$18.0~\%; seven rose, five fell). Against the mean of 2022--2023, the parish's 2024 gifts are 6.3~\% lower and its revenue 4.8~\% lower, where the peers' medians are $+$5.8 and $+$5.1~\%; on each measure the parish ranks ninth to eleventh of the thirteen. The difference between the parish's change and the peers' median is thus about seven points on gifts and eleven on revenue, inside the spread of the peers' own changes, whose middle half spans 31 and 25 points and whose full range runs from $-$50 to $+$73~\%.

\subsection{Detection threshold: how large a change would stand apart}\label{sec:threshold}

The year-to-year variation observed before the lane sets what this record can detect. Two designs are gauged: the parish against its own history (design A: the percent change of a line from one year to the next), and the parish against its neighbours (design B: the parish's change minus the median change of the twelve peers the same year). For each, the noise is the standard deviation of the eight pre-lane changes, 2015--2016 to 2022--2023, computed twice: on all eight, and on the five that exclude the three transitions marked by the pandemic (into 2020, into 2021 and into 2022). The threshold is set at two standard deviations: a change beyond it would be unusual at about the 5~\% level if changes were normally distributed and independent; with eight observations this is a gauge, not a test. The lane covered seven of the twelve months of 2024, so a full-year lane effect appears in the 2024 return at seven twelfths of its size; the full-year effect detectable in 2024 is the threshold times twelve sevenths. One full year (the 2025 return) is detectable at the threshold itself; the mean of two full years at the threshold divided by $\sqrt{2}$. The script is \texttt{derived/detection\_threshold.py}; its table:

\begin{center}\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{\input{detection_threshold_en.tex}}\end{center}

Read on gifts, design B without the COVID transitions, the most favourable combination: a lane effect would have had to remove about 25~\% of the parish's full-year gifts to stand apart in 2024; the observed difference from the peers' median is $-$7 points. Read on the parish alone with all eight changes, the least favourable: about 45~\%. Two full post-lane years at the same noise would bring the detectable effect to about 10 to 18~\% of gifts.

What limits the comparison. Twelve parishes is a small set, and several carry movements of their own in these years: one has other revenue of \$400,000 to \$700,000 in 2020--2022 and not since, one has gifts that halve in 2024 while its total revenue rises by 72~\%, two share an address. The returns record nothing of what happened at any of them (works, mergers, clergy, campaigns), nor at Sainte-Monique, whose 2024--2025 fundraising campaign (\$163,858 raised, by its bulletin of December 21, 2025) will appear in the 2024 and 2025 gift lines. The comparison says where the parish's 2024 figures sit among its neighbours'; it does not say why.

\subsection{What the bulletins say since the statements stopped}

Every bulletin in the parish website's archive was read for this note: 519 issues from May 2014 to October 18, 2026. Four findings. The statements did not resume: from April 30, 2023 to October 18, 2026 no bulletin carries a monthly or yearly statement, a balance sheet, a budget or a deficit figure; a job advertisement of September 2023 lists the issuing of monthly and year-to-date statements among a bookkeeper's duties. The parish's own account of its finances for 2024--2026 is a fundraising campaign for its 75th anniversary, « Preserving the Past, Building the Future », for « the repairs and maintenance of our Rectory and Church »: a donor tree of engraved leaves from October 2024; a table of December 21, 2025 giving, for 2024--2025, \$163,858 raised (events of 2024 \$35,605, events of 2025 \$28,674, general fundraising \$36,986, donor tree \$62,593), \$13,687 of expenses, \$149,516 remaining, and \$25,955 of major repairs in 2025 (a water leak in the rectory, \$14,955) with \$123,561 remaining; in April 2026 the campaign is said to have run « over the past three years », with a parishioner's pledge of \$25,000 to match contributions; in October 2026 the restoration of the church's columns is reported complete and the windows at the back of the church replaced. The gift lines of the 2024 and 2025 returns therefore include a capital campaign that the return does not separate from ordinary giving. One event carries the parish's name in its own: the bulletins of November 3, 10 and 17, 2024 announce that « the Save St.~Monica's Support Group with the St.~Monica's Fundraising Committee will be holding a rally in the church basement, Sunday November 24th immediately after the 11am mass », with four performing acts, free admission, « a donation at the door » and tax receipts for donations over \$25; the announcements give no reason for the rally, the group's name appears in no other bulletin, no bulletin reports what the rally raised, and the fundraising table of December 2025 gives the events of 2024 as one total (\$35,605 raised). No bulletin mentions the bike lane: across the 519 issues the words bike, bicycle and cyclist occur once, in a February 2019 column on a « green church » suggesting a bike rack and carpooling, and « Terrebonne » occurs only in the parish's address; no bulletin attributes a hardship to the lane or the street works. Counted on the issues published after the lane was built: in the 117 bulletins dated from July 1, 2024 to October 18, 2026, there are no references to the bike lane, none to financial difficulties associated with it, and none to parking difficulties; the word « parking » occurs three times, in February 2026, for free parking at a course held outside the parish. The silence is not that of a bulletin confined to spiritual matters: the same issues carry practical and civic notices, among them the parking arrangement of October 2023, the bookkeeper vacancy of September 2023, the fundraising accounts and the repairs reported above, Development and Peace campaigns asking parishioners to sign for federal legislation (October--November 2024) and a petition (March 2025), the wardens' elections, and, on October 19, 2025, a notice headed « Municipal Election -- Make Your Voice Heard! » stating that « local officials make decisions that affect housing, snow removal, parks, safety, and community programs » and that residents of Côte-des-Neiges--Notre-Dame-de-Grâce should be « consulted and heard on local issues that matter », with the voting dates. Nor do the bulletins give a count of the congregation: deaths appear only as occasional notices and in the mass intentions, baptisms and weddings only as standing office instructions, and the one series the bulletins do carry is the number of young people confirmed or making their first communion each spring (confirmations: 28 in 2014, 38 in 2015, 21 in 2016, 24 in 2017, 33 in 2018, 35 in 2019, 28 in 2022, 25 in 2023, 23 in 2024, 17 in 2025, 18 in 2026; first communions: 24, 23, 25 in 2014--2016, 30 in 2019, 19 in 2022, 29 in 2023, 22 in 2024, 14 in 2026), a measure of the faith-education program rather than of attendance; the pastor's letter of September 7, 2025 says that « our Mass attendance remained steady » over the summer, without a figure.

\subsection{The parish's Facebook page}

The parish's official Facebook page (« St.~Monica's Parish Montreal », 814 followers) was searched on October 23, 2026 with the page's own post search, for bike, lane, path, parking, piste, Terrebonne, petition, rally, « save St.~Monica » and the neighbouring centre's name. Four posts concern the lane. Three date from the temporary lane of 2020: on July 25, 2020, « an appeal to our parishioners to take note that if this bike path proceeds as planned, there will be no parking on either side of Terrebonne from Belmore to Oxford. Many of our parishioners are elderly or have reduced mobility. There will be no place to park for people attending weddings or funerals, not to mention Sunday Mass », with a link to a petition and an invitation to write to the borough mayor and the district councillor (seven reactions, four comments, two shares); on August 31, 2020, a link to the city council's question form on « the upcoming bike lane extension from Madison to Cavendish »; on September 4, 2020, a link to the borough council's question form for the sitting of September 8, where « a motion proposed … for its removal » was to be heard, with the note that « as I pointed out at the Press Conference, the church is deeply concerned about the environment and wants our streets to be safe and healthy for everyone », followed by a citizens' committee's instructions for questions and e-mails. The fourth is the only post since the 2024 lane: on August 14, 2024 the page shared a Religion News Service article of August 13, 2024, « Would Jesus park in a bike lane? For Philadelphia churches, it's a real question », with the caption « An article relevant to many of our parish communities in Montreal! » (one reaction, one comment). The article is not a protest against bike lanes: it reports that Tenth Presbyterian Church in Philadelphia gave up its permit to park in a bike lane on Sunday mornings after eighteen weeks of cyclists' protests, its executive pastor telling members that neighbours « see us as self-centered », that the Philadelphia Ethical Society had moved its parking to a nearby park as « the right thing to do », that Old Pine Street Church had supported the bike lanes from the outset and obtained Sunday parking one block over, and that five of seven congregations along the route had found alternatives; a cycling-association director is quoted on meeting « in the middle ». The share thus set before parishioners an account of churches accommodating a bike lane. Since the protected lane was built, this share is the parish's one official public comment on bike lanes; its 2020 posts on the temporary lane, which it opposed, already paired that opposition with the statement that the church « is deeply concerned about the environment and wants our streets to be safe and healthy for everyone ». No post mentions the parking arrangement of 2023, the rally of November 2024, or a financial effect of the lane. On parking: on October 15, 2023 the parish announced that « through the kindness of the MacKay Center, our next door neighbour, we have secured additional parking spaces for those attending Mass at St.~Monica's Church during the weekends on Saturdays and Sundays throughout the year », in the Centre's side parking area, with dashboard passes for « a minimum one time donation of \$5.00 »; the notice was repeated until December 3, 2023 and does not appear afterwards. The arrangement predates the lane by seven months.
