**[00:00] Henry:** Residents in Côte-des-Neiges-NDG have elected a new borough mayor. Stephanie Valenzuela captured just half, 50% of the vote, defeating incumbent Gracia Kasoki Katahwa. **[00:20] Valenzuela:** Good morning. Thank you for having me. How are you feeling? To be honest, still really overwhelmed with the support I've been receiving in the last few days and the messages, but feeling very good and very proud of the work that we put in during this campaign.

**[00:40] Valenzuela:** Oh, really, it's the door knocking and the meeting the residents and hearing everyone over the last 42 days of this campaign. But even before that, when we announced our complete team in August, I've been a sitting councillor for the District of Darlington for the last four years. And for me, in this mandate, I really learned the importance of meeting with residents, hearing them out. What are the grievances? What are the issues they'd like to resolve? And that's really what I took with me along this campaign. How to listen to people, how to bring solutions. And I think with that, the results are there. It shows that that's exactly what we did in this campaign.

**[01:16] Henry:** So let's talk about solutions and the next four years. What are your top priorities for the borough?

**[01:16] Valenzuela:** Yeah, well, we were very clear from the very beginning when we announced our local team and our campaign. We are very focused on bringing back basic services in our borough, taking care of the development of housing and the preservation of our housing stock in the borough, making sure that we stay affordable as much as possible. And of course, you know, security has always been top of mind in the borough of Côte-des-Neiges-NDG. So these are three, you know, axes that are very clear to us that we want to make sure we prioritize. But at this very moment, it's the budget of 2026 that we're already starting to work on to make sure that we have these priorities in there.

**[02:12] Valenzuela:** So there was two social off-market projects that were adopted in the last council with the council that we had, you know, prior to the campaign and to the election. There's potentially a lot of different scenarios that are going to come in with recounting across the city. But we have a plan and we have every intention of working with all the stakeholders, including the Gallo [Gallo [GALOPH]] and the local stakeholders that we have here in the borough.

**[03:08] Valenzuela:** There are definitely plans to do something with that. That was something that was also announced as one of our, you know, campaign engagements with Soraya. But I'll give you a very concrete example of something that needs to advance just in terms of local housing stock. There's a project called Project Chester on Walkley. It has been abandoned since, I believe, 2012. There's potential to already start working on that project. And so we need to prioritize that. So that's one of the first things that I'm going to be doing coming into my seat as borough mayor is to make sure that we can finally advance on this project. If there's money that is missing, if there's, you know, funding that is needed, we need to find a way to work with our different partners to finally advance on this project.

**[04:03] Henry:** Another thing that was talked about during the campaign was extending Cavendish.

**[04:03] Valenzuela:** Yes.

**[04:03] Henry:** What's the plan going forward with that?

**[04:03] Valenzuela:** Well, as we had announced just a few weeks ago already, towards the tail end of the campaign, our intention coming into this mandate is to make sure that we go forward with Cavendish Extension by deposing our intentions through the BAP. You know, it will determine the actual way that we can do this in a way that is not hopefully detrimental to the environment. So we want to put that on the table, understand the feasibility of putting in the Cavendish Extension. But I think for us, our party, it's been very clear that if we're going to develop an entire site, like the NNamur-Hippodrome site, we need to make sure that accessibility is included, security is included. And with the SIM, it was very clear that you cannot develop a site with over 10,000 units and so much circulation and people going through if you don't have different access points that are safe and secure to enter the area. **[05:21] Valenzuela:** Exactly, yes.

**[05:21] Valenzuela:** Well, they've always been very close collaborators on this intention. My colleague, Alan de Souza from Saint-Laurent had been working with them for over, you know, decades. So if it comes to a point where we have to sit down and discuss further, then for sure we will do that. But for now, we have to focus on the first step, which is deposing this project at the BAP.
