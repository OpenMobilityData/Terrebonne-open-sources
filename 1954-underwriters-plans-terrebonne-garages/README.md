# Off-street parking on rue de Terrebonne as built: the 1954–55 fire-insurance plans

**Issuer of the source:** Underwriters' Survey Bureau, Limited (Toronto and Montreal), *Insurance plan of the city of Montreal*, volume 7, sheets 739-2, 739-3, 739-4, 739-5, 775, 784-1, 784-2, 785-1, 787-5, 787-6, 787-7, 789-12, 789-13, 789-14, 789-15, 789-16 and 798-3. **Dates on the sheets:** « Montreal, vol. 7, November 1954 » (most sheets), « April 1955 » (775), « December 1955 » (739-4). **Scale:** 100 feet to 1 inch (sheet 785-1 at 200 feet to 1 inch). **Source of the scans:** Bibliothèque et Archives nationales du Québec (BAnQ numérique), collection « Plans de villes et villages du Québec », notice 0000174390, 273 files, permanent link [ark:/52327/2244199](https://collections.banq.qc.ca/ark:/52327/2244199); downloaded by the author in 2024 (file names `174390_<sheet>`). BAnQ's rights statement on the record: [Copyright Not Evaluated](https://rightsstatements.org/page/CNE/1.0/?language=fr). **Derived work:** this folder, October 25, 2026; counts, tables, maps, a note and a web page by the repository's author.

**Present-day check (October 25, 2026):** on the Gouvernement du Québec's continuous aerial imagery, with the City's parcels overlaid, 123 of the 126 buildings were read for a driveway, paved apron or parking pad visible today: **79 show one (64 %)**; built with a garage *or* with a driveway today: **89 of 123 (72 %)** (north 70 of 89, south 19 of 34; by dwellings 221 of 303, 73 %). Of the 1954–55 garages, 64 still have a visible apron, 10 do not; 15 buildings with no garage in 1954–55 have a driveway or pad today. What a garage holds inside cannot be seen from above.

## What the plans show

Each sheet draws every building with its construction, number of storeys and civic number, and writes *Auto* or *Autos* on every garage: in the basement (*Auto Bt*, *Autos Bast*), attached, or detached in the yard. Driveways without a garage are not marked. Each sheet carries the printed notice:

> This plan is the property of the UNDERWRITERS' SURVEY BUREAU, LIMITED, (unauthorized reproduction prohibited) and is loaned on the following conditions: that the plan is to be kept in good order, that it is to be used only in connection with business of Companies, Members of the Canadian Underwriters' Association and is to be returned on request to the UNDERWRITERS' SURVEY BUREAU, LIMITED, Toronto and Montreal.
>
> COPYRIGHT REGISTERED AT OTTAWA BY THE UNDERWRITERS' SURVEY BUREAU, LIMITED, TORONTO AND MONTREAL

Sheet 787-8 (south side of Terrebonne between Hampton and Melrose, the Royal and Draper blocks) is missing from the copy available and from every other copy found.

## The scans

The full sheets are not copied here: BAnQ serves all 273 files of volume 7 at the link above, and a reader is better served by the archive's viewer than by a second copy. The map with the 1954–55 underlay reproduces the frontage strip of each sheet at reduced resolution, as the evidence behind the count. On the status of the plans: the sheets print « Copyright registered at Ottawa by the Underwriters' Survey Bureau, Limited » (1954–55); BAnQ's statement is « Copyright Not Evaluated »; the author took legal advice in October 2026 and was told that reproducing them is not an issue. Sheet 787-8 is absent from BAnQ's file list too (787-1 to 787-7 only), which confirms the gap.

## Method

1. **Reading.** Every building on the Terrebonne block faces, both sides, from the west end of the protected lane (Belmore) to avenue Girouard, was read from the scans at two to four times magnification into `derived/buildings_1954.csv` (one row per building: sheet, side, block, cross streets, civic numbers, building type, the street it is addressed on, corner lot, garage yes/no, kind of garage, presumed access, confidence, notes). The unit is the building with a Terrebonne civic number; a semi-detached pair is two buildings. Corner buildings addressed on the cross street are listed and left out of the share, as are the churches, the presbytery and the vacant frontages.
2. **Georeferencing.** Each sheet is fitted to the City's géobase (CC-BY 4.0) by a similarity transform on the pixel positions of its Terrebonne intersections (`derived/controls.csv`, `derived/georef.py` → `sheet_transforms.json`); residuals 0–4 m (one at 7 m). Checked by drawing the City's assessment-roll parcels over each sheet.
3. **Join to the City's roll.** Each row is matched to the *unités d'évaluation foncière* (CC-BY 4.0) by civic number and street, or by position for corner buildings renumbered on the cross street since 1954 (`derived/buildings.py` → `derived/buildings_geo.csv`, with the unit id, position, dwelling count and year of construction). Unmatched rows are placed schematically along their block face (`match` = schematic).
4. **Present-day check.** Each block of the street is rendered in the same street frame from the Gouvernement du Québec's continuous aerial-imagery service (WMS `Imagerie_Continue`, layer `Imagerie_GQ`, servicesmatriciels.mern.gouv.qc.ca; the most recent provincial mosaic as served on October 25, 2026, leaf-off, about 8 cm per pixel; the service states no fee or access constraint and does not state the acquisition date), with the City's parcels, civic numbers and the 1954–55 markers overlaid (`derived/ortho_strips_qc.py`; the strips and tiles are kept outside the repository, the imagery is served and not redistributed). Each building was then read for: a driveway, paved apron or parking pad on the lot (`driveway_today` yes/no/unclear), a vehicle parked on it at the moment of the photograph, a garage door visible from above (never, in practice), the street it is reached from, a confidence grade and a note (`derived/present_day_check.csv`, 158 rows; `merge_present_day.py` joins them onto `buildings_geo.csv`). The south side between Hampton and Melrose, missing in 1954–55, was read today only (`derived/present_day_south_787-8.csv`: the eight duplexes of the Hampton–Royal face all show an apron; the Royal–Draper face is an apartment block with no drive on Terrebonne). The City's 2015 airborne LiDAR (open data, CC-BY 4.0; tiles 293-5035, 293-5036, 294-5035, 294-5036, 294-5037, 295-5037; classes ground, vegetation, building) was cropped to the corridor and rasterized with pdal (height above ground at 0.2 m, surface and ground elevation, ground-return intensity at 0.25 m; `pipeline_corridor.json`, `pipeline_ground.json` beside the tiles) and rendered per block in the street frame by `lidar_strips.py`. What it adds, seen on the strips: a vehicle parked on a lot stands out as a 1.3–1.8 m object on the apron, a detached garage as a 2.5–3.5 m box, and the ramp to a basement garage as tight 0.25 m ground contours, all independent of the optical reading and of the roll; its limits are the leaf-on 2015 flight (tree crowns hide parts of many yards) and its date (one summer in 2015). It was used here as a check on the optical reading, not as a third full reading; a systematic LiDAR reading (vehicles and garages per lot by height class) is the next step if the figure is contested.
5. **Cars parked at the moment of each acquisition.** On the aerial imagery (daytime, leaf-off spring, after June 2024: the lane markings are visible) the cars at the Terrebonne curbs were counted by eye per block and side: **158** (north 30, south 128). In the City's 2015 LiDAR (the files carry GPS week seconds, so the day of week and hour are known but not the date: the west end to about Walkley flown Sunday–Monday night 02:40–03:50 local, Walkley to Girouard Monday–Tuesday night 00:00–01:50, both weeknights; trees in leaf, so late spring to early autumn) the car-shaped objects at the curbs were counted by eye on the height-above-ground raster, block by block: **183** (north 93, south 90). An automatic detector (`derived/lidar_cars.py`: returns 0.9–2.3 m above ground not classed building or vegetation, rasterized at 0.25 m, car-sized connected components, rows of touching cars split by length; `derived/lidar_cars.csv`, one row per object) finds 85 of them, a recall of 46 % (`derived/lidar_detector_check.csv`, all 29 blocks; misses under tree crowns and in rows of touching cars, no false positive seen at the curbs), plus 253 car-sized objects on private lots within 45 m and 70 on the cross streets in the same band (detector only: lower bounds of the same order). The by-eye counts are the ones reported for the curbs. Figures: the two counts by block and side (`terrebonne_on_street_cars`), the detections on today's parcels (`terrebonne_parked_cars_lidar_map`), and panel by panel the daytime image above the night height raster with the detections drawn (`terrebonne_parked_cars_strips`), so that each square can be checked and the counts redone. A close-up (`terrebonne_parked_cars_closeup_<block>`, `cars_closeup.py`) draws every detection on one block as a box of its measured length and width and shows one curb car and one driveway car at 40 px/m with their heights (a flat top at 1.4–1.5 m over about 4.5 m); street versus lot is decided by distance from the street axis (2.2–7 m = curb), with the parcels drawn to show the lot objects in driveways and yards. The night count bears on resident parking; in 2015 the street had parking on both sides. The two counts are ten years and a redesign apart: each describes its moment.
6. **Tables, maps, note, page, slides.** `derived/overview_chart.py` (distribution by block face), `derived/cars_chart.py`, `derived/method_figures.py` (the three sources aligned, whole street and one block), `derived/maps.py` (three maps and their per-panel PDFs), `derived/build_note.py` (note and page), `derived/build_deck.py` (beamer, EN and FR). On every map and chart the dashed red line marks the side where the lane is to be converted to parking each winter under the proposal, as the councillor described it (the lane running with traffic: north side west of Cavendish, south side east of it). `derived/summary.py` (→ `summary.json`, `summary.txt`), `derived/maps.py` (both maps, EN and FR; the 1954 underlay needs the scans), `derived/build_note.py` (the note in LaTeX → PDF and `index.html`).

## Results (October 25, 2026)

| | Buildings addressed on Terrebonne | With a garage as built | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| North side | 90 | 57 | 63 % |
| South side (sheet 787-8 missing) | 36 | 18 | 50 % |
| **Both sides** | **126** | **75** | **60 %** (59.5) |
| High-confidence readings only | 97 | 60 | 62 % |
| **Today (aerial imagery, Oct 25, 2026): driveway, apron or pad visible** | 123 checked | 79 | 64 % |
| **Built with a garage or a driveway today** | 123 | 89 | 72 % |
| Dwellings in the same buildings (City roll, 121 buildings matched) | 324 | 192 | 59 % |

Of the 75 garages: 46 basement, 14 detached, 15 attached or in an annex; the door opens on Terrebonne for 64, on either street for 10 corner lots, on a rear lane for 1. Also on the block faces: 28 corner buildings addressed on the cross street (13 with a garage), 2 churches, the presbytery, 2 vacant frontages. Years of construction from the roll: 118 of 121 matched buildings date from 1955 or earlier (median 1932).

**Checks on the method** (note, section « Checks on the method »): georeferencing residuals under 3.3 m on 14 of 16 sheets (4.3 m and one 7.0 m point on the other two), checked against the City's parcels on every sheet; the 1954–55 reading agrees with the author's independent June 2024 marks on all 126 buildings; the present-day reading is one reader's, graded row by row, re-readable from public imagery, and was made before the conversion side was drawn on any figure; the LiDAR car detector, checked by eye on all 29 blocks, found 85 of 183 car-shaped objects at the curbs (46 %; `derived/lidar_detector_check.csv`), so the by-eye count is the one reported and the detector's lot and cross-street counts are lower bounds; 150 of 161 rows matched to the roll; a census of the frontage, not a sample; every number scripted from public inputs.

**Caveats:** garages, not driveways (the 1954–55 figure is a lower bound for "driveway or garage as built"); as built, not as used: the present-day reading sees aprons, driveways and parked vehicles from above but not what a garage holds inside, so neither figure is the share of households that park off-street today; old garages may be too small for today's larger vehicles; buildings, not households; one sheet missing in 1954–55; readings graded medium or unclear are flagged row by row.
