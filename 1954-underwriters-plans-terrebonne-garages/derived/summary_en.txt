Off-street parking on rue de Terrebonne as built: a reading of the 1954–55 fire-insurance plans. Summary (v2026-11-12-10-45-30).

Of the 126 residential buildings with a rue de Terrebonne address between the west end of the protected lane (Belmore) and avenue Girouard on the sixteen sheets available, 75 were built with a garage, that is 60 % (north side 57 of 90, 63 %; south side 18 of 36, 50 %). Counting only the readings made with high confidence gives 62 %. Weighted by the dwellings the City's assessment roll lists in the same buildings, 58 % of the 289 dwellings are in a building that was built with a garage.

The plans show garages as built, not driveways: a driveway without a garage leaves no mark, so the share of properties with a driveway or a garage as built is at least this figure. They show the stock of 1954–55, not of today; the City's roll dates 117 of the 120 matched buildings to 1955 or earlier (median 1932), so the frontage drawn is essentially the one standing today, but the use of each garage and driveway since then is not recorded here.

A street-level check of every building, made by the author on November 12, 2026 from street-level imagery (one lot at a time, without sight of any earlier reading), recorded whether at least one private place is usable today. It replaces a reading of the Gouvernement du Québec's aerial imagery made on October 25, 2026, because a check of the wider corridor from street level had shown that a view from above misses driveways. Of the 118 buildings judged (4 could not be told; one house of 1954–55 now stands inside a school lot and is left out), 99 have car access today (84 %; by dwellings 87 %). Counting a building if it either was built with a garage or has car access today gives 110 of 118, 93 % (north 83 of 86, south 27 of 32). On 28 buildings the parking has been turned to another use, wholly (12) or in part (16): a driveway fenced into a yard, a garage made into a room, a basement-garage ramp filled into a front pad. Garage interiors are not counted. (The aerial reading had found a driveway or apron on 79 of 123, 64 %, and garage or driveway on 89, 72 %.)

Both remote-sensing sources also show the cars parked on rue de Terrebonne itself. On the daytime imagery (undated, leaf-off spring, before the 2024 lane: cars still parked at both curbs) there were 158 cars at the Terrebonne curbs, 30 on the north side and 128 on the south. In the City's LiDAR, flown on two weeknights in 2015 between midnight and four, before the lane, there were 183 car-shaped objects at the curbs (north 93, south 90), counted by eye on the height raster; an automatic detector finds 85 of them. The figures show every object counted; both moments predate the lane; the imagery is undated, the LiDAR is from 2015.
