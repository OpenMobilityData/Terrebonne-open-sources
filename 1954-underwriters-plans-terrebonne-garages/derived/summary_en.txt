Off-street parking on rue de Terrebonne as built: a reading of the 1954–55 fire-insurance plans. Summary (v2026-10-25-15-37-03).

Of the 126 residential buildings with a rue de Terrebonne address between the west end of the protected lane (Belmore) and avenue Girouard on the sixteen sheets available, 75 were built with a garage, that is 60 % (north side 57 of 90, 63 %; south side 18 of 36, 50 %). Counting only the readings made with high confidence gives 62 %. Weighted by the dwellings the City's assessment roll lists in the same buildings, 59 % of the 324 dwellings are in a building that was built with a garage.

The plans show garages as built, not driveways: a driveway without a garage leaves no mark, so the share of properties with a driveway or a garage as built is at least this figure. They show the stock of 1954–55, not of today; the City's roll dates 118 of the 121 matched buildings to 1955 or earlier (median 1932), so the frontage drawn is essentially the one standing today, but the use of each garage and driveway since then is not recorded here.

A second reading, on the Gouvernement du Québec's aerial imagery (leaf-off spring, after June 2024, about 8 cm per pixel, with the City's parcels overlaid), found a driveway, paved apron or parking pad today on 79 of the 123 buildings checked (64 %). Counting a building if it either was built with a garage or shows a driveway today gives 89 of 123, 72 % (north 70 of 89, south 19 of 34; by dwellings 73 %). What a garage holds inside cannot be seen from above.

Both remote-sensing sources also show the cars parked on rue de Terrebonne itself. On the daytime imagery (spring, after June 2024, with the lane in place) there were 158 cars at the Terrebonne curbs, 30 on the north side and 128 on the south. In the City's LiDAR, flown on two weeknights in 2015 between midnight and four, before the lane, there were 183 car-shaped objects at the curbs (north 93, south 90), counted by eye on the height raster; an automatic detector finds 85 of them. The figures show every object counted; the two moments are ten years and a redesign apart.
