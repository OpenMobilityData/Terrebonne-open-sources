The Underwriters' Survey Bureau's insurance plans of Montreal (volume 7, sheets dated November 1954 to December 1955) draw every building on rue de Terrebonne and write Auto on every garage. Reading the sixteen sheets available building by building, from the west end of the protected lane (Belmore) to avenue Girouard, gives the figures above. The plans record garages as built, not driveways, so a property with a driveway and no garage is not counted: the share with a driveway or a garage as built is at least 60 %. They record 1954–55, not today: the City's roll dates 118 of the 121 matched buildings to 1955 or earlier (median 1932), so the buildings are the ones standing today, but what each garage or driveway is used for now is not recorded here.

The frontages today

A second reading, on the Gouvernement du Québec's continuous aerial imagery (fetched October 25, 2026, leaf-off, about 8 cm per pixel, with the City's parcels overlaid), recorded for 123 of the 126 buildings whether a driveway, paved apron or parking pad is visible on the lot today and from which street it is reached. 79 buildings (64 %) show one; counting a building if it either was built with a garage or shows a driveway today gives 89 of 123, 72 % (north 70 of 89, south 19 of 34; by dwellings 73 %). Of the garages of 1954–55, 64 still have a visible apron and 10 do not; 15 buildings with no garage in 1954–55 have a driveway or parking pad today. A vehicle was parked on the lot of 34 buildings when the photograph was taken. What a garage holds inside cannot be seen from above.

Cars parked on the street, in two acquisitions

Both sources show the cars parked on Terrebonne itself. The aerial imagery is daytime, leaf-off spring, after June 2024 (the lane markings are visible): 158 cars at the Terrebonne curbs, counted by eye (north 30, south 128; 90 on the side to be converted). The City's LiDAR was flown in 2015, before the lane, at night (00:00–04:00 local by the GPS times, trees in leaf): car-sized objects detected automatically number 85 at the Terrebonne curbs (north 38, south 47), 253 on private lots within 45 m of the street and 70 on the cross streets in the same band. The night count is the one that bears on resident parking; in 2015 the street had parking on both sides. The automatic count misses cars under tree crowns and may take a hedge or a shed for a car, so the lot figure is an upper bound. Two moments ten years and a redesign apart: not a before-and-after.

Where the frontage is

Of the 75 garages, 46 are basement garages, 14 detached in the yard and 15 attached or in an annex; the door opens on Terrebonne for 64, on either street for 10 corner lots and on a rear lane for 1. A further 28 corner buildings stand on the Terrebonne block faces but are addressed on the cross street, with their doors and driveways there (13 of them have a garage); they are shown as squares and left out of the share. Two churches, the presbytery and two frontages vacant in 1954–55 are shown as diamonds.

Caveats

  • Garages, not driveways. A driveway or parking pad without a garage leaves no mark on the plans; the figure is a lower bound for "driveway or garage as built".
  • As built, not as used. The 1954–55 figure counts structures; the present-day reading sees aprons, driveways and parked vehicles from above but not what a garage holds inside (a workshop, storage, living space). Neither is the share of households that park off-street today. A garage of 1930 or 1950 may also be too small for a large vehicle of today.
  • Buildings, not households. A duplex pair has two to four flats and one or two garages; three of the four apartment buildings have a basement garage of unrecorded capacity. The share by dwelling (59 %) happens to match the share by building.
  • One sheet missing (787-8, south side between Hampton and Melrose) and 29 readings graded medium, noted row by row in the table.

How the three sources were used

The whole street in the three sources, aligned in the same street frame (west half, then east half): the Québec government's aerial imagery, the City's 2015 LiDAR as height above ground, and the 1954–55 sheets. PDF

One block, Hingston to Hampton: paved aprons and parked cars on the optical image; the same cars and fences as 0.5–3.5 m objects and the houses as taller blocks in the LiDAR (red squares: the automatic car detections, at night in 2015); the « Autos Bast » marks of 1954. Another block, Rosedale to King-Edward, where the detached rear garages of 1954 are the 2.5–3.5 m boxes in the LiDAR.

Method and files

One row per building read on the scans (buildings_1954.csv); each sheet fitted to the City's géobase by its Terrebonne intersections (controls.csv, georef.py; residuals 0–4 m) and checked against the City's parcels; each row joined to the City's assessment roll by civic number and street, or by position for corner buildings renumbered since 1954 (buildings.py → buildings_geo.csv, with dwellings and year of construction); the present-day reading on the Québec government's aerial imagery rendered block by block with the parcels overlaid (ortho_strips_qc.py; the imagery is served, not redistributed), recorded in present_day_check.csv and joined by merge_present_day.py; maps by maps.py; this page and the note by build_note.py. The scans are BAnQ's (Insurance plan of the city of Montreal, volume 7, notice 0000174390, rights statement Copyright Not Evaluated) and are consulted there rather than copied here; the City data are CC-BY 4.0.

The author is a resident of Côte-des-Neiges–Notre-Dame-de-Grâce; counts and positions are the author's reading and may contain errors, which the row-by-row table is meant to let anyone find.
