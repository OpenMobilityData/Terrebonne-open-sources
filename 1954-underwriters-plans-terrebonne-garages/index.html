The Underwriters' Survey Bureau's insurance plans of Montreal (volume 7, sheets dated November 1954 to December 1955) draw every building on rue de Terrebonne and write Auto on every garage. Reading the sixteen sheets available building by building, from the west end of the protected lane (Belmore) to avenue Girouard, gives the figures above. The plans record garages as built, not driveways, so a property with a driveway and no garage is not counted: the share with a driveway or a garage as built is at least 60 %. They record 1954–55, not today: the City's roll dates 118 of the 121 matched buildings to 1955 or earlier (median 1932), so the buildings are the ones standing today, but what each garage or driveway is used for now is not recorded here.

Where the frontage is

Of the 75 garages, 46 are basement garages, 14 detached in the yard and 15 attached or in an annex; the door opens on Terrebonne for 64, on either street for 10 corner lots and on a rear lane for 1. A further 28 corner buildings stand on the Terrebonne block faces but are addressed on the cross street, with their doors and driveways there (13 of them have a garage); they are shown as squares and left out of the share. Two churches, the presbytery and two frontages vacant in 1954–55 are shown as diamonds.

Caveats

  • Garages, not driveways. A driveway or parking pad without a garage leaves no mark on the plans; the figure is a lower bound for "driveway or garage as built".
  • As built, not as used. Garages turned into workshops, storage or living space, driveways turned into gardens, front yards paved since 1955: none of this is recorded here. A garage of 1930 or 1950 may also be too small for a large vehicle of today. A present-day check of each frontage would be the next step.
  • Buildings, not households. A duplex pair has two to four flats and one or two garages; three of the four apartment buildings have a basement garage of unrecorded capacity. The share by dwelling (59 %) happens to match the share by building.
  • One sheet missing (787-8, south side between Hampton and Melrose) and 29 readings graded medium, noted row by row in the table.

Method and files

One row per building read on the scans (buildings_1954.csv); each sheet fitted to the City's géobase by its Terrebonne intersections (controls.csv, georef.py; residuals 0–4 m) and checked against the City's parcels; each row joined to the City's assessment roll by civic number and street, or by position for corner buildings renumbered since 1954 (buildings.py → buildings_geo.csv, with dwellings and year of construction); maps by maps.py; this page and the note by build_note.py. The scans themselves are not reproduced in the repository (see the folder's README on their status); the City data are CC-BY 4.0.

The author is a resident of Côte-des-Neiges–Notre-Dame-de-Grâce; counts and positions are the author's reading and may contain errors, which the row-by-row table is meant to let anyone find.
