## What the Fall 2025 deck says

**Program and scope.** « In 2022, we launched a major initiative to expand the citywide bike network, close key gaps, and increase the percentage of residents within a short walk from a safe and connected bike route » ([p. 2](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=2)). The seven projects completed in 2024: Boylston Street (Back Bay), Berkeley Street (South End, Back Bay), Milk Street (Downtown), North Beacon Street, Western Avenue and Winship Street (Allston/Brighton), all « Separated bike lane », and South Street (Allston/Brighton), « Contraflow street and speed humps » ([p. 3](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=3)). « In this evaluation, we looked at **user counts** as a key metric to measure project impact. Since these projects strategically expanded our bicycle network, we specifically analyzed bicycle and motor vehicle volumes. We compared counts taken before the projects (September 2022) with counts taken one year after the projects finished (September 2025). » ([p. 4](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=4)).

**Method.** Baseline counts Tuesday–Wednesday, September 27–28, 2022; post-installation counts June 11–12 and September 16–17, 2025, « mid-week dates that represent typical traffic conditions », « timed to allow at least six months of adjustment following project installation »; « Data collection was performed by a third party vendor using automated counting technology »; consecutive dates averaged; « data were not collected at every location on all of the dates listed »; « Control location data were derived from the Quarterly Bicycle Count program » ([p. 5](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=5)). Each result slide gives the two-day average and the range of the two days.

**Key takeaways (the deck's own).**
- Bicycles: « We counted **over 3,000 more** daily bike trips, **a 55% increase** across all projects. In our prior report on Better Bike Lane projects completed in 2023, we recorded a 44% increase in volume »; « Bicycles as a share of all traffic **increased to 7%** of daily traffic across all projects, **nearly doubling** from 4% in the baseline condition » ([p. 7](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=7)). By project (bikes a day before → after, change): Boylston 615 → 1,127 (+83 %), Berkeley 240 → 340 (+42 %), Milk 508 → 698 (+37 %), North Beacon 165 → 266 (+62 %), Western 538 → 812 (+51 %), Winship 97 → 145 (+49 %), South 74 → 90 (+22 %) (same page).
- Motor vehicles: « Motor vehicle volumes **decreased a modest -5%** across all projects while accommodating a major growth (+55% on average) in bicycling »; « The mixed results indicates that project impacts on vehicle volumes were localized »; « Measured increases in motor vehicle volumes, such as the +6% at Boylston Street, can be attributed to localized changes like the finishing of nearby construction projects » ([p. 8](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=8)). By project: Boylston 8,478 → 8,977 (+6 %), Berkeley 10,375 → 10,280 (−1 %), Milk 2,927 → 2,929 (0 %), North Beacon 11,114 → 10,556 (−5 %), Western 11,627 → 9,902 (−15 %), Winship 4,940 → 4,725 (−4 %), South 2,087 → 1,744 (−16 %) (same page).
- Controls: « Project streets measured an average **increase of +55%** in bicycle volumes, compared to +5% at the control streets group »; motor vehicles −5 % at project sites against −3 % at control streets; « The projects achieved major growth in bicycle trips without clear impacts to motor vehicle volumes »; « Fifteen control streets were selected for comparison. These streets had similar characteristics to the project streets and geographic coverage, but did not receive any bicycle infrastructure improvements or new connecting bike routes between Fall 2022 and Fall 2025 » ([p. 9](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=9)).

**Passages on motor traffic, diversion and the curb.**
- Boylston Street (separated lane, « 3 thru lanes to 2 thru lanes + turn lanes where needed », [p. 11](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=11)) against the parallel Commonwealth Avenue: « Neither location's volume changed enough to suggest that the vehicle capacity reduction on Boylston Street caused motor vehicle traffic to divert to Comm. Ave. The differences observed are **likely within the range of normal variation** » ([p. 19](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=19)). At Boylston west of Dalton, motor volume « **increased 50%** from 2022 to 2025. This growth is likely due to the completion of a nearby construction project » (the Dalton Street Bridge, [p. 18](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=18)).
- Milk Street: the only passage on parking. Motor volume « **decreased by 11%** … This reduction reflects the conversion of Milk Street from two-way operation to one-way westbound. The eastbound movement, which had a much lower volume than the westbound movement, was restricted to create a short-term parking and delivery zone. This expected reduction in volume was key to managing demand for food delivery and short-term parking while making space for the two-way separated bike lane » ([p. 32](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=32)). The deck contains no count of parking spaces removed or kept, and no occupancy measurement, for any project.
- Western Avenue, 2016–2025 at a project and a control location: « Unlike bicycle volumes, **motor vehicle volumes showed a general downward trend** at both locations from 2016 to 2025 »; « Motor vehicles dropped distinctly in 2020 due to the COVID-19 pandemic and have **remained below pre-pandemic levels ever since** » ([p. 46](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=46)); at Western west of Waverly, motor −18 % with bicycles +23 %, « notable considering the growth in residential and employment density along the corridor » ([p. 44](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=44)).
- Winship Street, a separated lane in one direction (uphill) and a shared lane in the other: « Southbound (bike lane) travel **increased by 56%** (+23 trips) … Northbound (shared lane) travel also **increased by 45%** (+25 trips) » ([p. 50](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=50)); motor vehicles −4 % ([p. 51](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=51)).
- South Street, « One-way for motor vehicles, Two-way for bicycles, No marked lanes », with speed humps ([p. 52](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=52)): contraflow bicycles 12 → 28 a day (+139 %), with-flow unchanged at 62 ([p. 55](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=55)); motor vehicles −16 %: « The reduction in motor vehicle trips (-343 / day) is much greater than the increase in bicycle trips (+16 / day) … The reduction in driving volume may be attributable to route diversion. The addition of speed humps in the corridor could have lead some drivers to select alternative routes » ([p. 56](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=56)).

**Conclusions and what is not measured.** « We can conclude that Better Bike Lanes projects built in 2023 and 2024 resulted in **immediate and substantial increases** in bike trips … Control data suggests that these are **actual increases** in the number of bike trips rather than existing trips diverting to the new facilities »; « We saw **no notable impact on motor vehicle trip volume on average**, though some corridors experienced greater variation. Determining whether these changes were due to local factors, regional trends, or our projects directly will require further analysis » ([p. 58](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=58)). Listed as future work: « **Volume trends** … Analyze seasonal volume patterns »; « **Safety** … (requires multi-year data) … motor vehicle speeds »; « **Operations**: Analyze vehicular travel times and routing to evaluate the project's impact on corridor-level operational efficiency and regional travel »; « **Qualitative**: Gather user feedback via surveys and review public comments » ([p. 59](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/other-cities/2025-boston-better-bike-lanes-evaluation/raw/Better_Bike_Lanes_Fall_2025_Eval_Web_Version.pdf#page=59)). The deck measures daily volumes on two days in June and two in September; it contains no travel-time, delay, parking-supply or parking-occupancy measurement and no winter count.

## Why it is here

First entry of the `other-cities/` collection ([`../README.md`](../README.md)): a city's own before-and-after evaluation of protected lanes and a contraflow street on arterial and local streets, with control streets, on the two quantities most often raised about rue de Terrebonne, bicycle volumes and motor-vehicle volumes. What it does and does not measure is stated above; any comparison with Terrebonne belongs in a note in `derived/` or in the private working papers, not in this README.
