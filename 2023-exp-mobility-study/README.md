## What it is

The mobility study commissioned by the borough before the 2024 reconfiguration of rue de Terrebonne (one-way street with protected bike lanes in both directions, St-Ignatius to Girouard). According to the borough's press release of October 30, 2023: the firm was mandated by the borough council in September 2022; the study cost $150,000 before taxes, with a $50,000 contribution from Infrastructure Canada's Active Transportation Fund; EXP carried out new counts of vehicles, bicycles and pedestrians at 17 intersections and characterized more than 1,100 parking spaces, the counts and parking surveys being made in October 2022; the study area runs from Somerled to Monkland and from St-Ignatius to Girouard.

## What it says about a bidirectional lane

On report page 51 ([PDF p. 62](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/2023-exp-mobility-study/raw/EXP_Etude_de_mobilite_rue_de_Terrebonne_2023.pdf#page=62)), under « Contraintes et enjeux » (keeping on-street parking, no major roadworks planned, driveways, short blocks with many intersections, on-street drop-off zones), the study records:

> « Pour ces raisons, il est convenu en amont de l'élaboration des concepts et de concert avec l'arrondissement qu'un aménagement bidirectionnel n'est pas souhaitable dans le contexte de la rue de Terrebonne. En effet, une telle configuration comporte plusieurs conflits et enjeux de sécurité, notamment avec la présence de nombreuses entrées charretières et avec la gestion de plusieurs intersections. »

The three concepts EXP then developed (section 6.1.2) are accordingly all one-way cycle lanes or shared lanes: A, one-way traffic with a contra-flow cycle lane and a shared lane; B, one-way traffic with a one-way cycle lane on each side (retained by the borough and built in 2024); C, two-way traffic with shared lanes, conditional on cutting through traffic below 500 vehicles a day. No one-side two-way lane is evaluated anywhere in the study. The other occurrences of « bidirectionnel » in the report concern two-way motor traffic, the Walkley and Saint-Jacques lanes as context, a generic design typology (section 5.3.3) and a traffic-calming table.

## Why it is here

The 2026 record released under access request 2026-581 (see the companion repository) refers to this study's traffic projections and to the "two mandates" question: the 2023 study was a feasibility and design study for a project the borough had decided to pursue, and the 2026 note was commissioned to recommend a change to that project. Readers of either analysis should be able to open the 2023 study itself.

**Publication and public meeting (from the release itself):** the release of October 30, 2023 states « Aujourd'hui, l'arrondissement publie les résultats de l'étude et dévoile le scénario choisi », and announces exchanges with institutional partners in the week of November 20 and « le 30 novembre, lors d'une rencontre citoyenne s'adressant à la population générale ». The borough's decisional file for the February 5, 2024 approval (1246880002, section « Opération(s) de communication ») records: « Une séance d'information citoyenne a aussi été faite le 30 novembre 2023. »
