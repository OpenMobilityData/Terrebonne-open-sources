Lay summary

The survey asked people who live on rue de Terrebonne or within a block of it about their parking: whether they keep a car, whether they have a place off the street, where the car was parked last night, and how hard winter parking has been. The answers are counts of the people who replied, not measurements of the street. This note sets them beside the measurements that do exist.

The borough's own 2023 study counted the parking places and measured how full they were on two days in the autumn of 2022. It found room everywhere: on the Thursday evening, half of the places on Terrebonne were empty, and so were about 298 places on the first 50 metres of the cross streets. A night-time laser survey of the city in 2015 shows 183 cars parked along Terrebonne, about one place in three, fewer in the west and more in the east. Both of those counts, taken before the bike lane, are close to the number of places that remain on the one side of the street that still has parking today.

More than eight in ten homes on Terrebonne have at least one private parking place, by a building-by-building check from street level. Survey respondents living on Terrebonne reported one much less often, about half of the households with a car; respondents within a block reported one somewhat less often. For the street itself the gap is larger than a sample of 35 explains: households without off-street parking were more likely to answer, or some respondents did not count a driveway.

60 respondents from the corridor had parked on a street the night before, 15 of them on Terrebonne itself, and 35 described real difficulty in winter under the survey's strict definition. Those are floors: there are 573 dwellings on Terrebonne and about 5,029 within a block, and the survey reached one home in fifteen on the street and one in seventy nearby. The conditions the difficulty questions describe, snow removal pending or under way, happened on about 32 nights a winter over the last five winters, 23 of them nights of snow loading itself, with stretches of three weeks or more without any every winter.

What no one has measured is how full the street is now, with the lane in place and parking on one side. The borough's own study of 2023 is the last measurement of occupancy; the next, commissioned in October 2026, is due on November 27 and counts the supply only.
