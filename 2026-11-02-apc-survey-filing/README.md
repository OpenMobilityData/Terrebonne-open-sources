# Survey on winter cycling and parking on rue de Terrebonne: the results as handed to the borough council on November 2, 2026 (corrected November 3)

**Issued by:** the Association des piétons et cyclistes de NDG (APC-NDG), letter signed by Jason Savard, with the survey's methods note in both languages. The survey was run by the association on Google Forms from October 15 to November 2, 2026; the methods note states who ran it, what it asked, what it did not collect, how the answers were counted and the changes made to the forms, with their times.

**Date:** handed over at the sitting of the borough council of Côte-des-Neiges–Notre-Dame-de-Grâce on November 2, 2026 (item 10.08, correspondence; minuted at that sitting, the minutes to appear in the bundle of the next sitting). A corrected version was sent to the borough clerk on November 3, 2026: in the first version the sentence on the comments asking for no bike path at all read « zero »; the correct figures are four comments, three of them among the 161 responses kept. The files here are the corrected version.

**Obtained:** copies kept by the author, who prepared the counts for the association from the survey's exports. The bundles contain no personal information: no name, address, e-mail or telephone number was collected, and the counts are aggregates.

**Link status:** not applicable (documents filed on paper and by e-mail).

## What the letter reports (its own figures)

Between October 15 and November 2, 2026, 205 people answered; the counts are limited to the 161 respondents who live on Terrebonne or within one block, or who cycled on Terrebonne last winter. 87 cycled on Terrebonne last winter, 36 at least weekly; 27 said a child rides on Terrebonne in winter; asked how safe they would feel cycling in the traffic lane beside parked cars, 80 of the 87 said less than safe. 108 live on Terrebonne or within a block; 59 had parked on the street the night before, 34 of them reporting winter parking hardship under the letter's strict criterion. On the preference question, 91 of the 161 chose to keep the protected lane in both directions, 57 to convert one direction to parking for the winter and 12 to allow parking only during snow-loading operations; the 44 excluded split 17, 19 and 7; four comments, three among the 161, asked for no bike path at all, an option the question did not offer. The letter states that the sample is not representative and that the figures are counts of respondents, not estimates of prevalence.

The anonymized answers and the full report are to follow, per the association's methods note.
