**James Bugden** (7:36:50)  
Good evening. My name is James Bugden. I live in Melrose, in Côte-des-Neiges, Notre-Dame-de-Grâce. My question is for Mr. DeSouza. On February 10th, the city launched its Cycling Network Review with the mayor saying Montrealers felt they had not been listened to. Results are due by year's end after a consultation this fall. On October 6th, the borough announced that one direction on the Terrebonne bike lane will become winter parking with change before Christmas. Its stated purpose is parking, not safety, and city documents suggest that the weakest data was used and the city engineers have resisted. NDG residents who vote overwhelmingly for Peter McQueen were promised they will be heard. Our councillor has been excluded from this entire process. Changing the lane now means ignoring our choice and risk contradicting the city's own finding. Will the city ask the borough to wait for the review it promises before changing the Terrebonne lane?

**Chair (council president)** (7:38:24)  
Alexandre, Monsieur Teodoresco.

**Councillor Teodoresco** (7:38:24)  
Madame la Présidente, I'll take the question. Thank you, James, for your question. It's sad that we didn't see you yesterday at Café Citoyens where 40 of my residents were able to discuss, among other things, the Terrebonne bike path. But let's rest assured that this is not the only topic of discussion in our borough. And as it seems, social media is not real life, and the discussion in person was very cordial and reasonable. And when we have time to express ourselves in a proper manner, then we can find a proper compromise. And I think this is the path that we're trying to find with our administration, a very pragmatic, reasonable approach. I know this is not the approach that was pushed forward by our predecessors, and that's really unfortunate because they really polarize the population in a way that is not constructive for our population, and even is not constructive for active mobility in the borough. And this is definitely our goal here, is to find a proper compromise that will enable safe mobility for all, and also more confident in quality of life in a way that we respect our residents who express themselves before the elections and after, saying that they need change. And just on the data, the question of the weak data, we have years of daily counting data from counters across the borough and the city. It's not weak data. We've done a recent campaign. We went door to door, 4,000 doors. So this is not weak data. And like I said, we're looking at when it matters the most. And it's those only those four months during winter, when we're talking about 3.6% of overall cycling traffic, right? It's 3.6% that we're talking about only for four months, and then the rest we protect and we keep as is. Thank you very much, James.
