            a. Schoolagendafor 2025-2026
                   Daniel asked the question about whether a paper agenda should be reinstated. He
                   suggested a paper agenda would help students organize their work and help parents
                   keep track.

        Silvana: Staff must be consulted and parents need to make a case one is needed.
        She asked whether the idea was for paper or virtual. There would be an added fee
        for paper.
        Lynn: The paper agenda becomes an environmental issue and is time consuming
        regarding some age groups and abilities. Agendas can be forgotten at school or
        home. Some children get nervous when the teacher writes in it. Emailing parents is
        very effective. She is a proponent ofonline agendas.
        Kathleen: The paper agenda was discontinued because parents didn't like it.
        (Agendas were forgotten, lost, etc.). At the time, when staff council voted, they
        wanted to streamline and make things more accessible and organized. The current
        system is working well, despite glitches. Also, some parents complain about too
        many emails. She prefers Google classroom and judges the paper agenda untenable.
        Daniela: There are too many emails as it is. She tries to keep her child off screens at
        home. Somehow the child needs to be responsible one. She said trying to go
        through all the Classrooms tiring.
        Laurice: We need to keep in mind the types of students. For some, either paper or
        Google Classroom will be difficult. Many parents are on top of it, tracking things.
        Also, some have a device, some don't; the same with a printer, but teachers can
        help those without the technology. She favours technology, where things can be
        found easily and in one place.
        Karen K.: At secondary school, where she works, the paper agenda is going strong.
         It's an equity issue. Some aren't using Google Classroom because it's not available at
        home. Teaches put their Classroom up on screen and students take notes.
         Karyn M.: prints from Google Classroom and puts papers in a binder. The search
        function is not handy. She feels it falls too much on parents to teach the agenda
        skills.
         Laurice: In Grade 6, there is a transition-to-high-school course, where students learn
         about time management and how to use an agenda. She said Cycle 3 students get a
         printed monthly calendar.
         Kathleen: She, along with some other teachers, provides a hard copy for projects.
         The agenda is a staff council decision because it's a teacher workload issue. She
         welcomes parent feedback. She asked to meet with a parent on GB to look at how
         Classroom appears for them.
         Daniel: No consensus among the GB. But could GB consider an online platform,
         where work is one central location. He finds the current situation untenable.
         Silvana: Let's re-evaluate Google Classroom. It will be discussed at staffcouncil.

           c.    Approval of Educational Project
                    Silvana: The EP is in place from 2023-2027. This year, it's a report on what is being
                    put into place, based on targets, baseline and June 2024 exams.
                    Objectives l to 3 include math, ELA, French subjects. Objective 4 School Climate
                    (based on school survey), she said the school hopes to bring the 23% of students
                    reporting feeling unsafe at school due to disrespect and exclusion (including
                    virtually) down to 21%. The percentage is within the norm ofthe national
                   percentage.
                   Objective 5 deals with outreach events for pre-school parents.
                   Objective 6: Aim to increase target to 70% from 59%, which means more skillset-
                   building for digital competencies.
Moved by Karen K. and seconded by Kristin. Adopted unanimously.


           d.  Approval of Anti-Bullying/Anti-Violence Action Plan (ABAV)
                   Silvana presented the ABAV
                       o All EMSB schools follow a template. Willingdon made an initial plan and this
                            year made adjustments. She showed GB the plan, which is posted on the
                            school website.
                   Daniel: commends the teachers for handling different situations. He expressed his
                   concerns about supervision when lunch monitors need to handle incidents.
                   He inquired about the cellphone policy at Senior Campus
                   He also asked if it's a school board policy to keep students' names private. As a
                   parent, it's helpful to know who is involved in the incident.
                   Silvana said supervision is always a concern and we continue to provide training and
                   monitor its efficiency. Re: devices: It's EMSB policy to not have cell phones exposed
                   in school. Re: sharing names: Usually parents of an aggrieved child know the name
                   of perpetrator, but the admin protects the name of the student when working on a
                   case.
Resolution: Requesting that the plan be approved as presented.
Moved by Lynn Grunberg and seconded by Kathleen. Adopted unanimously.
