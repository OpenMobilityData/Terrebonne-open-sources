Lay summary

How many cars belong to the households along the Terrebonne bike lane, and how much curb is there to park them? Québec's vehicle registry, as released, places each vehicle only in a postal district of about 10,000 households (H4A or H4B), not on a street. This note looks closer, and says which parts are measured and which estimated.

Measured: the curb. Using the City's inventory of parking signs, every side of every block was read for stretches where parking is always forbidden. The 22 census areas that touch the lane, reaching about one block to each side, hold 37 km of legal curb. That is an upper limit: driveways and hydrants are not removed.

Estimated: the cars. Census facts about each small area (renters, commuters who drive, household size, income) were used to share out each district's registered automobiles. For the corridor's 5,471 households the estimate is about 4,600 automobiles, between 4,400 and 4,900: 0.84 per household. For a single small area the estimate is uncertain by about a quarter either way.

Tested where the answer is known (sharing a group of districts' total among its members), the method was off by 5 per cent at the median, against 14 per cent for a split by number of households.

At 5.5 m per car, the corridor's legal curb would hold about 6,800 cars: more than the estimated number registered there, even if all were kept on the street. Every lot was examined on aerial photos and a random sample of 112 checked from the street: 85 of 99 small buildings have a driveway, pad or garage. That makes about 2,200 private places, garage interiors and apartment buildings not counted. Neither figure measures parking difficulty: visitors, time limits and distance to one's door are left out.

The note makes no recommendation.
