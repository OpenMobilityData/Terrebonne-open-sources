# How many people cycle rue de Terrebonne in winter, and how often? Bicycle counts on four sensors, a frequency distribution, and the APC-NDG survey in context (note, deck, data)

**Issued by:** the author of these repositories (see the root README), from four count series and two public documents: the borough's loop counter at Terrebonne/Kensington (hourly by direction, July 26, 2025 to May 11, 2026, released under access request 2026-300; daily totals to August 31, 2026, request 2026-581), the author's two Telraam S2 sensors at King Edward and Draper (hourly, daylight, August 2024 to May 2026; the counts are Telraam's, see [Terrebonne-telraam](https://github.com/OpenMobilityData/Terrebonne-telraam) « Data and licence »; only aggregates are published here), the City's detector det-00118 at Girouard/Terrebonne (open data « Compteurs cyclistes permanents », CC-BY 4.0; westbound only on Terrebonne; extract in `raw/`), the Miovision one-day counts of September 19, 2024 and January 29, 2025 (requests 2026-581 and 2026-634), ECCC daily weather for Montréal-Trudeau (2024 in `raw/`; 2025–2026 in `Terrebonne-access-requests/2026-300/derived/`), and the public figures of the APC-NDG survey filing of November 2, 2026 ([`2026-11-02-apc-survey-filing`](../2026-11-02-apc-survey-filing/)).

**Date:** analysis of November 7, 2026.

**Question.** The borough's winter figures are passage counts at one point; the survey counts people. What do the counts on every sensor, in the periods when they overlap, say about the number of individuals who cycle the street in winter and how often each rides, and how do the survey's 87 winter cyclists (36 at least weekly) sit against them? Trips are the unit of exposure and of the counters; people are the unit of the survey; the note keeps both and states every assumption that converts one into the other.

**Main findings** (details, intervals and caveats in the note): from December 1, 2025 to February 28, 2026 the loop counted 3,232 passages, about 42 per weekday and 20 per weekend day; about 11 matched morning–evening round trips per weekday make up half of weekday passages; 41 % of passages are in darkness. On dry days Draper carries 1.3 and King Edward 0.6 times the Kensington count, and Girouard westbound 1.6 times the loop's westbound channel: winter traffic concentrates toward the east end. On snow days the Telraam-to-loop ratio rises by 50–60 % at both Telraam sites, a discrepancy the data do not explain (camera over-detection, or cyclists leaving the snowed-in lane for the roadway where the loop cannot see them). Model-free floors: at least 154 distinct cyclists at King Edward on March 9, 2026 and 42 at Kensington on December 3, 2025, if no one passed more than twice. The band model identifies the passages of the frequent core and its round trips, not the head count without a frequency assumption: across four template sets the frequent core at Kensington is 11–85 people and the occasional tail 25–292. The survey's 87 would produce about 60 % of the Kensington count under central assumptions (17–152 % across the stated ranges): consistent with the count, and a floor under the winter riding population rather than a measure of it.
